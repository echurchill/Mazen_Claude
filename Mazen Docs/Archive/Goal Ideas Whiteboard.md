# Whiteboard — Goal / Objective Ideas (Claude's side)

*Brainstorm 2026-07-07, ahead of the goals conversation. Grounded in a quick pass of what's popular / acclaimed across mobile, tablet, and PC. Not decisions — options to react to.*

## What we uniquely have (the differentiator)

We are a **first-person, embodied** traveler **inside** a twistable maze-planet. Every close cousin — Captain Toad, Mekorama, Monument Valley, Fez — is played from **god-view**. Nobody is standing *at ground level* while the world reconfigures *around* them. Add the multi-world portals + the "real other world hanging in the sky" and we're in genuinely unoccupied space. **The novelty isn't the twist — it's being inside it.** Whatever goal we pick should lean on that.

Our verbs, today: **walk**, **twist** (rearrange the maze around you), **portal** (travel between worlds), **discover** (fog lifts). Bandaging adds **constraint** (some twists refused). That's a rich, unusual verb set.

## What the research says (proven genres our mechanics fit)

- **Diorama-navigation puzzle** — *Captain Toad: Treasure Tracker, Mekorama, Monument Valley, Evo Explores, Mirealle.* Navigate a small 3D world to a goal; rotate/reconfigure to open paths; cozy, no timers, tactile, collectibles. **The closest lineage to us** — and huge on mobile/Switch.
- **Rotate-the-world puzzle + exploration** — *Fez* (rotate a world to realign paths, explore, collect cubes, decode a hidden language/mystery), *Echochrome, Crush.* Our slice-twist *is* "rotate to realign paths."
- **First-person puzzle-exploration, no combat** — *Myst / Obduction* (portal-linked worlds, environmental puzzles, atmosphere), *The Witness* (an island of puzzles + epiphanies — literally reviewed as "a Rubik's cube wrapped in a mystery"), *Outer Wilds* (a tiny solar system you explore, knowledge is the progression), *Antichamber, Manifold Garden.*
- **Reconfiguration/rule puzzles** — *Baba Is You, Patrick's Parabox* (Parabox is Sokoban where blocks are *spaces you enter* — recursion that rhymes with our "bigger on the inside" portals!), classic Sokoban. This is the grammar bandaging speaks.
- **Cozy-puzzle success factors** (consistent across the market): *simple to learn, deep to interact; no timers/pressure; gorgeous minimalist visuals; the "a-ha!" moment; clarity + feedback; star→unlock progression.* (The match-3 megahits — Royal Match, Candy Crush — are a different, monetization-driven lane; not our DNA. Our lane is the "thinky/cozy" one: Monument Valley, The Room, Baba.)

## Five goal concepts to react to

### 1. **Wayfinder** — reach the beacon by reshaping the maze *(Captain Toad × Fez, embodied)*
Bite-sized levels. Each is a cube/region with a **destination** (a beacon / the way home). The route is broken; you **twist slices to open a path** from where you stand to the goal, then walk it. Optional collectibles hidden behind twists or in the fog. Bandaged structures are **walls you must route around** (can't cut them). No timer; escalating twist-complexity.
- **Fit:** uses the twist as the central verb; embodiment is the hook; proven, accessible genre.
- **Needs:** goal markers, a per-level win check (you can path start→goal), hand-authored levels, gentle onboarding.
- **Most accessible / most obviously "a game."**

### 2. **Restore the world** — solve the scrambled cube from the inside *(Rubik's × Myst)*
The world begins **scrambled** — paths severed, structures split (the house in halves). Goal: **twist it back toward a target** — realign the maze so a continuous path/pattern/picture completes across faces, reunite split structures, mend the broken world. Bandaging constrains the legal move set (a true bandaged-cube solve, but *inside* it). The **killer visual pays off hard**: watch the moon heal as you restore it.
- **Fit:** leans hardest into our Rubik's identity; bandaging is core, not bolt-on; the sky-worlds become progress meters.
- **Needs:** a "target state" definition + a solvability guarantee, a legible "how close am I" signal.
- **The most *us* — and the most novel — but the hardest to make readable.**

### 3. **The Traveler** — explore & uncover across worlds *(Outer Wilds × Myst × Fez)*
No fail state. The goal is **discovery**: roam the cube, the moon, house-interiors via portals; uncover secrets, lore, hidden chambers gated by twists and portals; collect artifacts (Fez) or unravel a mystery (Outer Wilds). Twists reveal hidden routes; the portals + sky-worlds are the wonder. Progression is **understanding**, not score.
- **Fit:** best showcase for the portals + killer visual; cozy, atmospheric, non-violent; ages well.
- **Needs:** authored content/secrets, a light narrative or mystery spine, a sense of "you found something."
- **The most atmospheric / highest ceiling, most content-hungry.**

### 4. **Shepherd** — guide someone home *(Mekorama × Lemmings × a pinch of heart)*
Guide a **companion** (a creature / a lost traveler / a rolling light) from A to B across the maze. You **twist to build or clear their route**; they walk it. Bandaged structures are fixed obstacles. An emotional hook (protect them) on top of the spatial puzzle.
- **Fit:** puzzle + warmth; a clear "why"; reads well in first- *and* third-person.
- **Needs:** companion pathfinding, fail/retry framing.

### 5. **Caretaker** — grow & shape the world *(cozy sandbox, Monument-Valley-calm)*
Tend the cube-world: reveal the maze, plant/grow structures, arrange it, **unlock decorations** by clearing gentle objectives. Twisting is *expressive*, not just puzzle. Soft collection/aesthetic goals, no fail.
- **Fit:** maximal chill, showcases the pastoral aesthetic + props.
- **Needs:** building/placement systems, a light objective feed. **Lowest "game," highest cozy.**

## My recommendation: a spine of **1 + 3**, with **2's ethos** as the depth

Lead with **Wayfinder (1)** as the moment-to-moment loop — reach the beacon by twisting open a path — because it's accessible, proven, and puts the twist front-and-center. Wrap it in **The Traveler (3)** as the meta: beacons are **portals onward**, so clearing a level *travels you to the next world*, and a light mystery ("why is the world coming apart? who left these gates?") pulls you through. Let **bandaging + reunite-what's-split (2)** be the puzzle *depth* and the emotional through-line — the world is *scrambled*, and every solved path *mends* a little of it, which you can literally watch on the moon/earth in the sky.

**One-line pitch:** *A cozy first-person puzzle-adventure where you're a tiny wayfarer on a cluster of maze-planets — twist the world around you to open the path home, travel between worlds through gates, and slowly mend a place that's come apart.*

**Platform instinct:** **tablet/phone-first** (a one-finger drag to twist a slice is exactly the tactile input Captain Toad/Mekorama/Monument Valley proved people love), with PC/Mac too. The embodiment reads great on a touch screen you can also orbit.

## Design principles to hold (from the cozy-puzzle research)
- **No timers, no pressure.** Reward patience; let people sit in it.
- **Simple to learn, deep to interact** — one verb (twist) that composes into hard problems.
- **Clarity + feedback** — always show the goal, what's locked (bandaged), and "you're closer." The refusal cue we're about to build matters here.
- **The a-ha, then a reward** — a small celebration + an unlock (a new gate, a new world, a mended piece) at each solve.
- **Gorgeous and calm** — we already have the pastoral look, the sky, the light. Lean in.

## Open questions for us to settle together
- **Authored vs procedural** levels? (Puzzle quality usually wants *authored*; our maze is procedural today — probably a hybrid: procedural texture, authored goals/constraints.)
- **Fail state or not?** (Cozy leans no-fail / infinite retry.)
- **How much narrative?** (From "zero, pure toy" → "light mystery" → "full Myst-style story.")
- **One planet or a cluster?** (The killer visual begs for *several* worlds you hop between.)
- **What does bandaging *mean* in-fiction** — ancient locked monuments? bonded ruins? — so "you can't cut here" reads as intentional, not arbitrary.
