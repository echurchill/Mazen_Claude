# Player Journey — A First Session (a tool to find the minute-to-minute)

*A step-by-step walkthrough of one imagined play session, written to drag the **minute-to-minute verb** out of the idea soup. Not canon — a probe. Verb annotations in **[brackets]**. Companion to the [Design Synthesis](Garden%20of%20Worlds%20—%20Design%20Synthesis.md).*

> The test: if this session is *legible* and *quietly compelling* with almost no UI and no one telling you anything, the loop works. Where it drags or confuses, the design has a hole.

---

## 0:00 — The threshold
You stand in a small pastoral clearing on your home world — hedges, a sandy path, a low sun. It's calm. There's nowhere obvious to "go," so you wander. **[walk]** Rounding a hedge you find a stone arch you've never seen, humming faintly, a soft light inside it. You step through. **[walk through a portal — no prompt, you just cross the threshold]**

Fade. You're somewhere else.

## 0:01 — Arrival, and the first "read"
A new world. Rounder than home — the ground curves gently up at the edges (**natural world; the round silhouette is telling you something, though you don't know what yet**). Green, wet, alive. Far overhead, another world hangs in the sky, slowly turning. You don't question it; you just feel small. **[look — the game is teaching you to *observe*]**

You walk. The maze here is looser, more like a garden than a labyrinth. **[walk / explore]** You round a corner and the path just… stops at a wall of hedge. Dead end. Or is it?

## 0:04 — The first twist (learned, not told)
On the ground where the path dead-ends, the paving stones carry a faint groove — a seam — running off under the hedge. You'd have missed it if you weren't looking. On a hunch you grab the world and **turn a slice** — one finger-drag / Q — and the whole strip of maze *rotates* around you, the dead-end hedge swinging away, a new stretch of path swinging in. **[twist — the core verb — discovered by noticing a seam, not a tutorial]**

The little jolt of *"oh — I can move the world"* is the hook. You do it a couple more times just to feel it. **[twist, playfully]**

## 0:09 — A locked thing
Deeper in the garden you reach a squat stone structure — mossy, obviously old, half-swallowed by the hedge. A temple, maybe. You try to twist the slice it sits on to swing it toward you — and it **won't move.** A soft resistance, a low tone, a faint red glow tracing four points on the structure. **[twist → refused. You just met a *lock* without being told what it is — the refusal cue *is* the teaching.]**

You circle it. **[observe]** Those four glowing points — they're not random. Three are lit steady; one flickers. And on a fallen slab nearby, a carved diagram: four dots, three filled, one open. **[read the environment — a Witness beat]**

## 0:13 — Understanding = the key
You realize the flickering point matches a little mechanism on the temple's face — a stone dial with the same four-dot mark. You'd walked past three identical dials elsewhere in the garden, each already "set." This one isn't. You go back, find the last dial (it's at the center of a small hedge-spiral you'd skipped), and — having now *understood* what it's for — you align it. **[the "verb" of learning here = noticing a pattern across the space and acting on it. No inventory, no combat — you *knew* something you didn't before, and knowing it is what acts.]**

Back at the temple, the fourth point steadies. The red glow goes warm gold. The lock is undone.

## 0:15 — Twist it open, step inside
Now the temple's slice **turns.** You twist it — and the structure *opens*, a seam splitting the mossy stone, the outside peeling to reveal a dark doorway. **[twist — same verb, now with a payoff: you're not rearranging a path, you're *opening a thing*]** You walk in. **[walk through — into an interior]**

The world flips. You're **inside a cube** now — a hollow stone chamber, staircases climbing every wall, rooms on the ceiling, a shaft of light from far above. Gravity holds you to whatever surface you're on. The pastoral calm is gone; this is old, deliberate, *engineered*. (The round natural skin outside; the hard cubic truth within — you *feel* the reveal before you can name it.) **[walk / explore — inverted-cube traversal]**

## 0:22 — The memory
At the heart of the chamber, on a plain pedestal, is a small still thing — a mote, a captured moment. You reach it and it *opens into you*: for a few seconds you see through someone else's eyes — a Builder, maybe — watching this very garden being *planted*, seeds sown by machines, a world being *made* to look natural. **[receive a memory — the knowledge beat]**

The memory doesn't just tell you a story. It **changes what you can see.** Back outside (later), hedges you thought were wild now show their planted rows; a "hill" reveals the machine-buried under it. Understanding rewrote perception. **[knowledge as transportation / perception]**

And — a new arch, dark until now, lights up at the garden's edge. You didn't find a key. You *understood* something, and understanding opened the way. **[a new destination unlocks — because you're smarter, not stronger]**

## 0:30 — The pull onward
You could leave through the new arch to the next world. Or you could go *back* to your home world, which you can see turning in the sky — and now, knowing what you know, look at it differently. You choose. Nobody tells you to. **[the loop closes and re-opens: every world both answers a question and asks a bigger one]**

## What the minute-to-minute actually is

Strip the session down and the moment-to-moment is a tight braid of just a few small actions:

1. **Walk & look** — read a calm, hand-legible space for *seams, glyphs, patterns, things-out-of-place.* (The "observe" of a knowledge game.)
2. **Twist** — reconfigure the maze around you: to *open a path*, and — the escalation — to *open a structure*. One verb, two stakes.
3. **Meet a lock** — a twist that's *refused*; the refusal itself poses a small puzzle: *what do I need to understand to undo this?*
4. **Understand → undo** — find the pattern/mechanism across the space; knowing it *is* the action that unlocks. (No inventory grind — the "item" is comprehension.)
5. **Enter** — twist the unlocked thing open and step *inside* (inverted-cube interior), where the natural skin gives way to engineered truth.
6. **Receive & re-see** — a memory/fragment that *changes what you can perceive*, and quietly opens the next destination.

**The one-sentence verb:** *notice → twist → hit a lock → understand → open → go inside → see anew.* The twist is the hands; understanding is the key; going inside is the reward; re-seeing is the progression. It's cozy (no fail, no timer), grind-proof (every beat is new), and Witness-taught (nothing above was a tutorial — it was a *seam*, a *glow*, a *carving*).

## Where the joy lives (design targets)
- The **"oh, I can move the world"** first-twist jolt.
- The **"I *understand* it now"** click when a pattern resolves — the a-ha, un-grindable.
- The **peel** — natural outside → engineered inside — every single time you break in.
- The **re-see** — walking old ground with new eyes and finding it was never what it seemed.

## Holes this probe exposed (for us to solve)
- **How legible is "the lock needs *understanding X*"** without a UI? The refusal cue + environmental clue has to thread a needle: mysterious but not obtuse. This is the hardest craft problem, same one The Witness/Outer Wilds spend their whole budget on.
- **Is the "understand" verb concrete enough?** In this session it's "notice a pattern across the space and align a mechanism." That's good, but is it *repeatable and deepening* across 4 worlds without becoming "find the switches"? Needs a real puzzle-grammar (candidate: a **Builder glyph/routing language** you slowly learn to *read*, à la Chants of Sennaar/Heaven's Vault — reading becomes the deepening verb).
- **Inverted-cube comfort** — flipping gravity on entry could disorient. Needs a gentle transition and clear "which way is down."
- **Where does memory-*editing* enter?** This session only *receives* memories. The "prune/forget to enable" idea (the selective-forgetting theme) hasn't shown up as a verb yet — it may be a later-game deepening, or a whole second pillar. Open.
