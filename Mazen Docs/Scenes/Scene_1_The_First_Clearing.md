# Gameplay Script Draft: Opening Sequence
## Scene 1, "The First Clearing"

**Status:** Canonical connected draft
**Purpose:** Introduce movement, observation, exploration, environmental repetition, and portal travel without tutorial text or overt exposition.

---

## Dramatic Function

The game opens not with an explanation, but with an apparent certainty:

> This is Earth.  
> This is morning.  
> There must be a way out.

Each of those assumptions will eventually become questionable.

The clearing functions as both **stage** and **sealed question**. The surrounding maze expands the stage without yet revealing the larger world. The repeated vases establish the first piece of visual grammar associated with the Builders, though the player initially interprets them as decoration, memorials, or markers.

The portal is the scene's first unmistakable impossibility.

---

# SCENE 1A: HOUSE LIGHTS

## WORLD STATE

- **Topology:** Exterior world
- **Appearance:** Earthlike
- **Roundness:** High enough that the world reads as a planet, though curvature should not yet be obvious from the playable area
- **Time:** Dawn
- **Weather:** Clear, still, cool
- **Fog of discovery:** Active beyond the immediately visible clearing
- **Initial twist access:** Disabled or unexplained
- **Player objective:** None displayed
- **Music:** None at first

## SET

A roughly square clearing is enclosed by tall stone walls.

The walls are old but not ruined. Their blocks are too large, too evenly fitted, and too free of vegetation to belong comfortably to any recognizable human period. They rise well above the player's reach and offer no obvious handholds.

Grass and low groundcover soften the floor of the clearing. Small irregularities suggest natural earth, but there are no insects, footprints, fallen branches, or signs that anyone recently passed through.

A narrow break interrupts the northern wall.

It is not framed as a doorway. It is simply a place where the wall stops, leaving a passage barely wide enough to invite entry.

In one corner of the clearing stands a small group of unusual **vessels**.

---

## THE VESSELS

The vessels resemble elaborate stone or ceramic vases assembled from several nested forms:

- broad circular bowls
- compressed spherical chambers
- narrow connecting stems
- flared collars
- stacked, rotationally symmetrical sections

They look partly ceremonial, partly mechanical, and partly botanical. Their silhouette suggests that several different objects have been threaded onto the same invisible vertical axis.

No opening is clearly visible.

Their material resembles the surrounding walls but is smoother, with faint green-blue traces in the grooves. From a distance they appear weathered. Up close, their surfaces seem almost untouched.

### PROP IMPLEMENTATION

- Imported model placed on a facelet
- Twist-safe and bound to its tile
- No interaction prompt
- Two subtle animation channels may be used:
  - an almost imperceptible rotation among selected layers
  - a slow internal shimmer or changing surface reflection

The motion should be subtle enough that the player may doubt having seen it.

### BUILDER PRESENCE

**Not identified.**

At this stage, the vessels read as scenery. Their repetition will later suggest that they are markers, observers, interfaces, or portions of a presence extending beyond the world.

---

# SCENE 1B: CURTAIN RISE

## OPENING IMAGE

The screen fades in gradually.

The player is standing near the center of the clearing.

The camera begins facing roughly north, but not directly toward the opening. The gap in the wall rests near the edge of the initial view, discoverable through looking rather than presented as an objective marker.

The sun has just risen above the eastern wall. Its light reaches only the upper stones at first, then spills down into the clearing.

Across the sky, the moon is descending behind the far wall. For a few moments, both celestial bodies are visible.

This creates an image of transition:

> one light arriving, another departing.

The player does not witness how they arrived.

There is no waking animation, falling sequence, portal closure, or explanatory voice. The scene simply begins with their presence already established.

## AUDIO CUE

At fade-in:

- soft morning wind
- distant birds, sparse and difficult to locate
- faint movement through grass
- no human activity
- no music

After the player first moves, a low tonal layer enters almost below conscious notice.

---

# SCENE 1C: FIRST BLOCKING

The clearing permits the player to:

- walk
- turn
- inspect the walls
- approach the vessels
- look at the sun and moon
- discover the northern opening

No instruction explicitly says to enter the passage.

The walls should visually discourage climbing rather than merely functioning as invisible barriers. Their height, smoothness, and slight inward overhang communicate that the way forward is horizontal, not upward.

If the player approaches a wall, they hear a faint change in ambient sound, as though the stone absorbs nearby noise.

If the player approaches the vessels, nothing dramatic happens.

One layered section may complete a tiny quarter-turn while outside the center of the camera's view.

When the player looks directly at it, it is still.

---

# SCENE 1D: THE NORTH PASSAGE

Crossing through the break reveals that the outer wall is much thicker than expected.

For several steps, the player moves through a short stone corridor. The bright clearing remains visible behind them, framed like the stage they have just exited.

The corridor opens into a maze built from the same fitted stone.

## REVEAL CUE

The maze is not shown all at once.

The player initially sees:

- one forward path
- one immediate branch
- several walls hiding the maze's full extent
- a strip of sky overhead

The maze occupies approximately three times the playable area of the original clearing.

The player should understand its scale gradually through movement, not through an overhead view.

---

# SCENE 1E: THE MAZE OF VESSELS

## SET DESIGN

The maze walls are slightly lower than the clearing walls, allowing the player to see the sun and occasional upper edges of distant structures, but not enough to navigate by sight alone.

The paths are broad enough to avoid claustrophobia. The maze should feel contemplative rather than threatening.

Its structure contains:

- several short false routes
- longer branches that appear promising
- one or two loops
- repeated visual landmarks that complicate orientation
- multiple dead ends

At every dead end stands another vessel.

Some are solitary. Others appear in pairs or small groups.

No two need be completely identical, but they share the same stacked axial grammar.

## PLAYER EXPERIENCE

The first dead end establishes the pattern.

The second makes the player notice it.

The third turns it into a question.

By the fourth, the vessels no longer feel like decoration. They feel placed.

The player may begin using them as navigation markers, only to discover that several look nearly identical.

### OPTIONAL SUBTLE VARIATION

Each vessel may react differently to the player's route:

- one rotates a middle ring
- one emits a barely audible tone
- one appears warmer in sunlight
- one carries a faint reflection of a place not visible nearby
- one remains completely inert

These differences should not yet form a solvable puzzle. They are the first syllables of a language the player does not know they are hearing.

---

# SCENE 1F: PASSAGE OF TIME

The maze exploration should last long enough for the opening celestial arrangement to change.

The moon disappears behind the walls.

The sunlight rises, gradually reaching deeper into the maze.

This produces a natural progression without a timer or spoken cue. The world itself marks the player's movement through the scene.

If the player returns to the original clearing, it remains unchanged except for the light.

The vessels remain where they were.

Or appear to.

---

# SCENE 1G: THE DIFFERENT THING

After enough false routes, the player enters a corridor that initially resembles the others.

The light changes first.

Colored illumination trembles faintly across the stone ahead, too saturated to be sunlight.

The ambient birds fall silent.

The low tonal layer that began when the player first moved becomes audible, though still quiet.

The corridor bends.

Beyond the turn stands an archway.

---

## THE ARCHWAY

The arch is constructed from the same stone as the maze, but its proportions are more deliberate. It is not ruined and bears no inscription.

Within it is not darkness or another corridor.

It contains a vivid, moving field.

The portal's surface suggests depth without showing a stable destination. Color folds inward, briefly revealing fragments that might be:

- stars
- foliage
- machinery
- ocean light
- a room seen from an impossible angle

None remains long enough to become certain.

Light from the portal spills across the floor and up the nearby walls. It also reflects from the vessel placed beside the arch.

This vessel is the largest encountered so far.

Its uppermost layer turns slowly toward the player.

Not like a head.

Not quite.

## PORTAL STATE

- Active
- Unsealed
- Trigger positioned at its center sub-cell
- Travel begins when the player deliberately steps into the field
- No automatic pull
- No interaction text unless required by accessibility settings

The player must choose to cross.

---

# ENDING IMAGE OF THE INTRODUCTION

From the final approach, the player can look backward.

Behind them lies the stone maze, sunlit and apparently terrestrial.

Ahead is the portal, vivid and physically impossible.

Beside it stands the vessel, silent and unreadable.

The scene ends not when the portal is discovered, but when the player enters it.

> **BLACKOUT:** None.  
> **TRANSITION:** The portal itself becomes the scene change.  
> **TRAVEL MODE:** Push to Scene 2 exterior world.  
> **NEXT WORLD:** Scene 2, "The Four Corners."

---

## Design Principle Established

This opening teaches the player four things without stating them:

1. **Exploration is the primary verb.**
2. **Repetition is meaningful.**
3. **Objects may be more than they appear to be.**
4. **Progress requires willingly crossing into uncertainty.**

The first apparent character the player encounters is not a person.

It may be a vase.
