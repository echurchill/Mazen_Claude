# Gameplay Script Draft: Scene 3
## "The Heart of the World"

**Status:** Canonical connected draft
**Purpose:** Introduce interior-world topology, six-face navigation, symbol matching across distant surfaces, cumulative activation, spatial orientation around a shared center, and portal creation at a newly designated destination.

---

## Dramatic Function

Scene 2 taught the player that a destination may already exist on a hidden surface.

Scene 3 goes further:

> The player is no longer standing on a world.  
> They are standing inside one.

The chamber initially resembles an enormous six-sided room. Exploration gradually undermines that interpretation. Every wall is also a floor. Every surface contains paths. Every obelisk points toward the same suspended center.

The puzzle asks the player to understand the interior as one continuous place rather than six separate walls.

The way forward is not located at a fixed doorway. It appears only after the player has awakened the entire chamber.

---

# SCENE 3A: DESCENT INTO THE WORLD

## WORLD STATE

- **Topology:** Interior world
- **External relationship:** This is literally the inside of the larger exterior world explored in Scene 2
- **World dimensions:** 5x5x5
- **Playable surface:** Six interior faces, each 5x5 tiles
- **Shape:** Hard-edged cube
- **Sky:** None
- **Lighting:** Lantern-like architectural illumination, no sky or celestial bodies
- **Fog of discovery:** Active
- **Player twist access:** Disabled
- **Scripted twists:** None during this scene
- **Portal arrival behavior:** One-way
- **Music:** Low harmonic texture, initially almost inaudible
- **Primary mechanic:** Match six remote plinths to six central obelisks
- **Exit portal:** Created only after all six obelisks are active

## EXTERNAL RELATIONSHIP NOTE

From inside, there is no sky.

The apparent ceiling is another physical, walkable interior face rather than a window to space.

The small orbiting world glimpsed in Scene 2 is not visible from here. It exists outside the larger world, above or around its exterior, while the player is now enclosed within that world's interior topology.

Scene 3 should therefore feel connected to Scene 2 by descent and containment, not by a visible horizon.

## TRANSITION FROM SCENE 2

The player enters the downward-flowing chamber between the two obelisks.

Light descends around them.

The outer world stretches upward while the player appears to remain still. Stone, sky, and the two obelisks elongate into vertical streaks.

For a brief instant, the player sees darkness crossed by six faint planes.

Then their feet meet metal.

The downward motion stops without impact.

The portal field remains active behind them for several seconds, illuminating the chamber in pale vertical bands.

It then collapses toward the floor.

The light narrows into a ring, contracts into a point, and disappears beneath the tile.

No portal structure remains.

The route back is gone.

---

# SCENE 3B: THE SIX-SIDED CHAMBER

## OPENING IMAGE

The player stands near the center of one interior face.

It reads naturally as the floor.

Four walls rise vertically around them. Far above is a ceiling constructed from the same broad tile pattern as the surface beneath their feet.

Each surface is five tiles across and five tiles high.

The chamber is perfectly cubic.

At first glance, the player appears to be inside a vast metal room.

That interpretation is only partly correct.

The "ceiling" is not a sky and not a transparent shell. It is the opposite interior face of the same cubic world, reachable through the maze.

## SET DESIGN

The six faces are covered by a continuous maze.

Its walls are assembled from a patchwork of metal cubes and rectangular blocks:

- dark iron
- tarnished brass
- dull steel
- oxidized copper
- blackened alloy
- occasional pale ceramic or crystalline inserts

The blocks vary slightly in size and age, but all conform to the tile topology.

Some sections resemble careful machinery.

Others look repaired, replaced, or accumulated across enormous spans of time.

The maze walls are not smooth partitions. They are built from joined metal units, producing an irregular mechanical relief.

Seams, rivets, inset plates, and occasional dormant light channels make the environment feel engineered rather than constructed by hand.

## LIGHTING

There is no sun.

Light comes from:

- narrow seams between selected wall blocks
- dim recessed fixtures
- weak reflections from the central crystal
- dormant obelisk markings
- occasional moving glints from somewhere beyond the player's current face

The ceiling is visible, but distant and muted.

The center of the chamber is the brightest point.

---

# SCENE 3C: THE SUSPENDED HEART

At the exact geometric center of the cube floats an orb or many-faceted crystal.

It does not touch any surface.

It remains motionless despite having no visible support.

## THE CENTRAL ORB

The object is large enough to remain visible from every face, but small enough that the distances around it remain imposing.

Its form is difficult to classify.

From one angle it appears spherical.

From another, its surface reveals shifting crystalline planes.

Fine internal structures rotate or refract independently, suggesting depth greater than its external volume should contain.

Initially, the orb emits only a faint internal glow.

Its light rises and falls slowly, almost like breathing.

It produces no beam and no interaction prompt.

The player cannot reach it.

## INTERIOR GEOMETRY CUE

Every surface of the chamber has its local "up" directed toward this shared center.

The orb therefore occupies the same position above the player regardless of which face they stand on.

As the player crosses from one face to another, the chamber reorients around them, but the orb remains the spatial anchor.

It is not on the ceiling.

It is always inward.

---

# SCENE 3D: THE SIX OBELISKS

In the middle tile of each interior face stands an obelisk.

There are six in total.

Each rises inward from its own surface and points toward the suspended orb.

None touches it.

The equal gaps between their tips and the orb create an invisible six-axis structure through the chamber.

## INITIAL STATE

All six obelisks begin inactive.

They are dark and silent.

Each obelisk carries one symbol repeated identically on all of its visible sides.

The repeated marking allows the symbol to be read regardless of the direction from which the player approaches.

## SYMBOL SET

Each obelisk has a distinct symbol.

The symbols should be visually related, suggesting a shared language, but easy to distinguish.

Possible design principles include:

- six variations of line and circle
- six different rotational symmetries
- six abstract representations of orientation
- six stages in a geometric transformation
- six shapes corresponding conceptually to the faces of a cube

The player is not given names for them.

For design documentation, they may be temporarily labeled:

- Symbol A
- Symbol B
- Symbol C
- Symbol D
- Symbol E
- Symbol F

These labels never appear in the game.

## INACTIVE OBELISK

- Symbol dark
- Surface matte
- No beam
- No sustained sound
- Crystal remains unaffected

## PLAYER INTERACTION

Activating an obelisk directly does nothing.

If the player attempts to interact:

- the symbol flashes faintly
- a distant answering tone sounds from somewhere else in the maze
- the obelisk remains inactive

This teaches that the control is located elsewhere.

---

# SCENE 3E: LEARNING TO WALK THE WALLS

## FIRST FACE CROSSING

The maze on the starting surface leads toward its edges.

There is no staircase, ladder, or gravitational transition.

When the player crosses the boundary between the floor and a wall:

- movement continues without interruption
- the player's orientation rotates smoothly
- the former wall becomes the new floor
- the former floor becomes a wall behind them
- the central orb remains above them toward the chamber's center

The transition should be readable but not theatrical.

The chamber is not changing.

The player is learning its true geometry.

## AUDIO CUE

At the first face crossing:

- a faint harmonic shift accompanies the rotation
- the sound field subtly reorients
- the central orb emits a single soft pulse
- no tutorial text appears unless accessibility options require it

## PLAYER UNDERSTANDING

The first crossing establishes that:

- all six surfaces are walkable
- face boundaries are not barriers
- the "ceiling" can be reached
- the chamber is one continuous maze
- the orb is the chamber's stable center

The world initially presented six architectural surfaces.

The player discovers one topological surface folded around an interior.

---

# SCENE 3F: THE SIX MATCHING PLINTHS

Hidden throughout the maze are six plinths.

Each stands in a dead end.

Every plinth carries one symbol matching one of the six obelisks.

## PLINTH PLACEMENT RULE

A plinth must not be placed on the same face as its matching obelisk.

Each pairing therefore requires the player to connect a remote control with a distant response.

The six placements should encourage broad traversal across the chamber.

A possible arrangement:

- A plinth on the north face controls the obelisk on the south face.
- A plinth on the east face controls the obelisk on the ceiling.
- A plinth on the floor controls the obelisk on the west face.
- The remaining pairings cross the chamber in similarly nonlocal ways.

The exact mapping may be authored for navigational rhythm, but should not follow an immediately obvious opposite-face rule for all six.

## PLINTH APPEARANCE

The plinths are made from the same patchwork metals as the maze, but their upper surfaces are smoother.

Each has:

- a recessed symbol
- a cylindrical activation cap
- a thin seam around the cap
- no text
- no directional indicator

All six begin inactive.

## ACTIVATION

When the player activates a plinth:

1. Its symbol illuminates.
2. The cylindrical cap rises.
3. A clear tone sounds.
4. A pulse travels away through embedded channels in the metal maze.
5. The matching obelisk activates elsewhere in the chamber.
6. The central orb briefly responds.

Unlike Scene 2, activation is not reversible during normal play.

Once raised, a plinth remains active.

The puzzle is cumulative.

---

# SCENE 3G: THE ANSWERING OBELISK

The player may or may not be able to see the matching obelisk when a plinth is activated.

The chamber communicates the response in several ways.

## DISTANT ACTIVATION CUE

- A bright pulse travels across seams in the metal blocks.
- The tone repeats from the direction of the matching obelisk.
- The central orb flashes once.
- A beam appears between the obelisk and the orb.

## OBELISK ACTIVATION SEQUENCE

At the matching obelisk:

1. Light appears in the engraved symbol.
2. The illumination spreads upward through narrow channels.
3. The obelisk's surface gains a faint metallic shimmer.
4. Its tip brightens.
5. A throbbing beam extends toward the central orb.
6. The beam makes contact with the orb's surface.
7. The orb answers with an internal pulse.

The obelisk does not move from its tile.

The beam bridges the empty space between the obelisk and the center.

## BEAM APPEARANCE

The beam is not perfectly steady.

It pulses in slow intervals:

> brightening -> narrowing -> dimming -> brightening

Its rhythm should feel alive without implying biological machinery.

Each activated beam has the same basic visual language, but may produce a slightly different tone.

As more beams connect, they form a partial geometric cage around the orb.

## PLAYER FEEDBACK AT THE PLINTH

If the matching obelisk is not visible from the plinth:

- the plinth's symbol remains illuminated
- a directional glow moves briefly along nearby wall seams
- the orb's response can be seen above the maze
- the new beam may become visible from an adjacent corridor or after the player crosses onto another face

The game trusts the player to seek the result.

---

# SCENE 3H: EXPLORING THE INNER MAZE

## MAZE DESIGN

The six-face maze should be understandable as one connected system while still allowing each face to possess a distinct texture.

Possible face characteristics:

- one face dominated by narrow metal corridors
- one with broad open routes and low maze walls
- one containing dense cubical machinery
- one showing heavy oxidation and repair
- one with reflective metal surfaces
- one with dark, nearly seamless construction

These distinctions assist orientation without labeling the faces.

## DEAD ENDS

Each plinth occupies a genuine maze dead end.

Other dead ends may contain:

- dormant machinery
- empty mountings
- patches of unusual metal
- partial symbols
- viewpoints toward the center
- evidence that structures have been replaced or repaired

Not every dead end contains a reward.

The player must learn to explore without assuming that every branch produces an immediate mechanism.

## ORIENTATION LANDMARK

The central orb remains visible from many locations.

Activated beams make it increasingly useful as a navigational reference.

The player may look upward and infer:

- which obelisks are active
- which faces have already been visited
- where a newly awakened beam originates
- how their current surface relates to the chamber as a whole

The puzzle state is therefore visible in three places:

- activated plinths
- illuminated obelisks
- beams connected to the central orb

---

# SCENE 3I: THE GROWING CONSTELLATION

Each activation changes the chamber's atmosphere.

## AFTER ONE OBELISK

The first beam reveals the scale of the interior.

The orb brightens slightly.

A low pulse becomes part of the ambient sound.

## AFTER TWO OBELISKS

The two beams illuminate suspended particles between the surfaces and the center.

Their rhythms begin to alternate.

## AFTER THREE OBELISKS

The orb rotates or refracts more visibly.

Faint lines appear inside it, suggesting an incomplete internal structure.

## AFTER FOUR OBELISKS

Dormant light channels awaken across portions of the maze.

The chamber becomes easier to navigate.

## AFTER FIVE OBELISKS

The five active beams briefly synchronize.

The remaining inactive obelisk flashes once.

Its symbol becomes more visible, helping the player identify the final matching plinth.

This is guidance, not an objective marker.

## BUILDER PRESENCE

The chamber does not speak.

Its state changes are its speech.

Each activation is both mechanical progress and a sentence added to an emerging geometric statement.

---

# SCENE 3J: THE SIXTH CONNECTION

When the final plinth is activated:

1. The plinth rises and locks.
2. The sixth obelisk illuminates.
3. Its beam reaches the center.
4. All six beams pulse independently for several seconds.
5. The chamber falls silent.
6. The beams synchronize.

The six active beams now connect the middle of every face to the central orb.

The orb is suspended inside a complete structure of light.

## COMPLETION IMAGE

From a suitable viewpoint, the six beams resemble three intersecting axes passing through the orb:

- floor to ceiling
- one wall to its opposite
- the remaining wall pair

The orb is not being supported.

It is being addressed from every direction.

## CENTRAL ORB RESPONSE

The orb's internal facets accelerate.

Its glow strengthens until the surrounding maze casts crisp shadows.

The six beams contract into narrower, brighter lines.

A wave of light travels outward from the orb, down each beam, into every obelisk, and across all six faces.

The wave reveals the full cube for a moment.

Then the chamber returns to its darker state.

The puzzle is complete, but the exit has not yet appeared.

---

# SCENE 3K: THE ORB CHOOSES A SURFACE

After a deliberate pause, the orb begins turning.

Its movement is unlike mechanical rotation. Different internal planes shift in different directions until one facet aligns with a tile somewhere in the chamber.

## DESTINATION RULES

The destination tile is selected from valid portal locations.

It must:

- not be on the player's starting face
- be reachable through the current maze
- not occupy a face edge or corner triple-point
- have enough clear space for the portal structure
- remain persistent once selected
- preferably encourage the player to cross at least one more face boundary

The choice may vary between playthroughs, but it should be fixed for the current world state once made.

## TARGETING BEAM

The orb emits a new beam.

Unlike the six broad, pulsing obelisk beams, this one is:

- narrow
- continuous
- sharply directional
- brighter at its point of contact

It strikes a specific tile on one of the non-starting faces.

The chosen tile illuminates.

The surrounding maze walls catch the glow, making the destination visible from several possible viewpoints.

## PLAYER EXPERIENCE

The beam functions as a diegetic destination marker.

The game does not display an arrow or waypoint.

The player can locate the portal by:

- following the beam visually
- identifying which face it reaches
- navigating toward the illuminated tile
- using the central orb as a reference while moving across surfaces

The chamber itself points the way.

---

# SCENE 3L: THE NEBULA GATE

## PORTAL EMERGENCE

At the targeted tile:

1. A seam appears around a concealed floor panel.
2. The panel divides or retracts.
3. A portal frame rises from the tile.
4. Its components unfold and lock together.
5. The orb's targeting beam strikes the portal's crown.
6. The interior field ignites.

The portal rises perpendicular to the local face.

From another surface, it may appear to stand sideways or upside down.

From the player's local perspective upon arrival, it stands upright.

## PORTAL APPEARANCE

The portal field contains a vivid nebula.

Its imagery includes:

- luminous clouds
- distant stars
- slow currents of colored gas
- dark voids suggesting enormous depth
- occasional flashes resembling stellar birth or collapse

It does not clearly show the destination.

The field behaves more like a window into deep space than a liquid surface.

Parallax within the nebula responds subtly as the player moves past it, implying impossible depth inside a frame only a few steps wide.

## AUDIO

The portal produces:

- a broad atmospheric hum
- low distant rumbles
- delicate high tones like signals from very far away
- no wind or suction

The six obelisk beams continue pulsing behind the player.

The seventh beam from the orb remains fixed on the portal until the player enters.

---

# SCENE 3M: THE FINAL APPROACH

The player must navigate from their current position to the chosen portal tile.

This final traversal is not another puzzle.

It is a spatial confirmation.

The player demonstrates that they now understand:

- every surface is walkable
- the orb marks the shared center
- distant surfaces can be reached by crossing face boundaries
- an object that appears sideways from one orientation becomes upright when approached

## OPTIONAL VIEWPOINT

The route should provide at least one position where the player can see:

- several activated obelisks
- multiple beams meeting at the orb
- the new targeting beam leaving the center
- the nebula portal standing on a distant face

This is the scene's grand tableau.

The chamber has become a luminous geometric instrument.

---

# ENDING IMAGE OF SCENE 3

The player stands before the nebula portal.

Behind them, the metallic maze wraps across walls, floor, and ceiling.

Six obelisks point inward.

Six throbbing beams converge on the central crystal.

A seventh beam extends from the crystal to the portal.

The apparent room has revealed itself as a world without a privileged floor.

The player steps through the nebula.

> **BLACKOUT:** None  
> **TRANSITION:** Nebular clouds expand beyond the portal frame and surround the player  
> **TRAVEL MODE:** Push outward to the small orbiting world above the Scene 2 exterior world  
> **NEXT WORLD:** Scene 4, "The First Turn"  
> **RETURN STATE:** The interior chamber remains persistent, with all six plinths and obelisks active

---

## Builder Presence

No Builder appears as a figure.

The six obelisks may be separate instruments, or six visible portions of one higher-dimensional mechanism.

The central orb may be:

- a control system
- a memory
- a navigational intelligence
- a Builder interface
- a local cross-section of something extending beyond the chamber

The player cannot yet distinguish among these possibilities.

The scene reinforces a developing pattern:

> The Builders do not necessarily communicate by speaking.  
> They arrange relationships and allow the player to complete them.

The six plinths are questions distributed through space.

The six beams are answers returned to the center.

The final portal is the chamber's reply.

---

## Design Principles Established

1. **Interior worlds have no permanently privileged floor.**
2. **All six faces form one continuous navigable surface.**
3. **The shared center provides a stable spatial reference.**
4. **Matching symbols can connect distant controls and mechanisms.**
5. **Puzzle progress may be cumulative rather than reversible.**
6. **Remote activations should produce visible and audible responses.**
7. **The chamber's central object can display global puzzle state.**
8. **A completed puzzle may designate a destination rather than immediately transporting the player.**
9. **Portal placement can vary while remaining reachable and persistent.**
10. **Objects that appear sideways from afar become locally upright when approached.**
11. **The environment communicates direction through light, geometry, and motion rather than interface markers.**
12. **The world's apparent architecture is only one interpretation of its topology.**

Scene 1 asked the player to cross a doorway.

Scene 2 asked the player to rotate a hidden doorway into view.

Scene 3 asks the world itself where the next doorway should be.
