# Gameplay Script Draft: Scene 2
## "The Four Corners"

**Status:** Canonical connected draft
**Purpose:** Introduce persistent device states, reversible interactions, remote status displays, symbolic communication, scripted world twisting, hidden surfaces, and travel into the world itself.

---

## Dramatic Function

The first scene taught the player that exploration can reveal a path beyond the apparent world.

The second scene changes that proposition:

> The destination is already here.  
> It is simply facing the wrong direction.

The player initially treats the maze as architecture surrounding an empty center. In reality, the maze is part of a larger mechanism. Its four corners control a hidden slice of the world, and the structure needed for further travel exists on a surface the player cannot yet see.

The player does not create the doorway.

They rotate reality until the doorway faces them.

---

# SCENE 2A: ARRIVAL WITHOUT RETURN

## WORLD STATE

- **Topology:** Exterior world
- **Appearance:** Ancient, rough-hewn, partially reclaimed by earth
- **World size:** Approximately twice the width and length of the previous maze
- **Time:** Daylight
- **Sky:** A blue star with about twice the apparent diameter of Scene 1's sun; no moon is visible
- **Orbiting body:** A small dark or angular world is visible high in the sky, unexplained and distant
- **Weather:** Dry and still, with occasional low wind
- **Fog of discovery:** Active
- **Player twist access:** Disabled
- **Scripted twist:** Enabled after puzzle completion
- **Portal arrival behavior:** One-way
- **Player objective:** None displayed
- **Music:** Minimal

## TRANSITION FROM SCENE 1

The player passes through the vivid portal beneath the stone arch.

There is no blackout.

The portal field fills the view. Its colors stretch into descending bands, then collapse into daylight.

The player steps into another clearing.

Behind them, the portal contracts into a bright vertical line, then a point, then nothing.

No archway remains.

There is only rough ground, scattered stone, and open air.

If the player turns immediately, they witness the disappearance. If they continue forward, they hear a brief inward rush and later discover that the route behind them is gone.

## AUDIO CUE

As the portal closes:

- Sounds from the first world vanish.
- A descending tone folds inward.
- The new world remains completely silent for several seconds.
- Wind gradually returns.
- Music begins only after the player approaches the central plinth.

The disappearance should feel quiet and final rather than threatening.

---

# SCENE 2B: THE CENTRAL CLEARING

## OPENING IMAGE

The player stands near the southern edge of a broad, irregular clearing.

Unlike the carefully enclosed starting area of the first scene, this clearing appears to have formed through collapse, erosion, or excavation.

Broken walls and scattered stones surround it.

The area seems to occupy the center of a much larger maze.

The walls nearest the center are incomplete:

- isolated stones
- low foundations
- leaning fragments
- gaps where corridors can still be inferred

Farther away, the maze becomes increasingly intact.

The progression is gradual:

> loose stones -> partial walls -> continuous walls -> massive outer corridors

At the outer limits, the walls appear nearly untouched.

The effect suggests that age, pressure, or some unknown force has radiated outward from the center.

The light is cooler and stranger than Scene 1. The blue star overhead casts pale blue highlights across rough stone, metal seams, dust, and shadow. No moon is visible. A small dark or angular world sits high in the sky, easy to miss at first and not yet explained.

## SET DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The maze must feel older than the structure in Scene 1.

The stone is irregular and fitted by weight rather than precision. Moss, mineral stains, packed soil, and exposed roots occupy its joints.

All wall dressing is derived from live maze topology so that the environment remains attached to its facelets during the eventual twist.

---

# SCENE 2C: THE APPARENTLY EMPTY CENTER

Directly ahead stands a single stone plinth.

Nothing stands behind it.

There are no visible obelisks, no chamber, and no obvious portal structure.

Beyond the plinth, the maze continues toward the far side of the world.

The absence should feel deliberate. The central clearing is large enough to suggest that something belongs there, yet the space remains unoccupied.

## THE CENTRAL PLINTH

The plinth is approximately waist-high and constructed from smoother stone than the surrounding maze.

Its upper surface bears a symbol divided into four quadrants.

Three quadrants contain solid dots.

The fourth contains a hollow ring.

```text
●   ●

●   ○
```

The arrangement corresponds spatially to four outer corner plinths hidden within the maze.

The player is not told this.

### SYMBOL LANGUAGE

- **Solid dot:** Corresponding corner plinth active
- **Hollow ring:** Corresponding corner plinth inactive

The markings appear inset into the stone rather than painted onto it.

When a distant corner plinth changes state:

- a dot hollows into a ring when deactivated
- a ring fills into a dot when activated
- a faint pulse passes through that quadrant

The central plinth is therefore not a map of the maze.

It is a map of conditions.

## EARLY INTERACTION

If the player activates the central plinth before all four corner devices are active:

1. The plinth depresses slightly.
2. The hollow ring flashes once.
3. A low, incomplete tone sounds.
4. Something deep within the far side of the world answers with a muted vibration.
5. The plinth rises back to its resting position.

No visible mechanism responds.

The feedback communicates that the central plinth functions, but something is missing.

---

## HIDDEN WORLD STATE

Unseen by the player, the far outer slice of the world carries another surface.

On the face currently turned away from the playable area are:

- two obelisks
- a cylindrical portal chamber between them
- a small surrounding platform
- maze connections that will become accessible after rotation

These structures are physically present from the beginning.

They are not spawned after puzzle completion.

They ride facelets on the hidden side of the rotating slice and remain inaccessible until that slice performs a quarter-turn.

While hidden:

- the obelisks are dark
- the chamber is dormant
- no portal field is present
- the structures produce no visible effect

The world is carrying its own exit behind its back.

---

# SCENE 2D: THE FOURFOLD MAZE

## MAZE STRUCTURE

Four broad routes extend outward from the clearing.

Age, rubble, and branching corridors conceal their underlying organization.

Each route eventually reaches one of the maze's four outer regions.

The maze contains:

- loops reconnecting near the center
- branches between quadrants
- false approaches to the outer boundary
- broken sections that later serve as shortcuts
- increasingly complete architecture toward the perimeter
- occasional high points from which the player can glimpse the far slice

The maze is approximately twice the width and length of the maze in Scene 1.

The player should understand that this is not simply a corridor between portals. It is a complete spatial problem.

## NAVIGATIONAL LANGUAGE

Each quadrant may carry a subtle environmental character:

- different mineral staining
- distinct vegetation
- characteristic arrangements of rubble
- slight differences in wind or ambient resonance
- differing degrees of wall preservation

These distinctions help orientation without turning the maze into four color-coded zones.

---

# SCENE 2E: THE CORNER PLINTHS

Each outer region contains a plinth resembling the one at the center.

Their top symbols distinguish them.

## CORNER IDENTIFIERS

- **First plinth:** One dot
- **Second plinth:** Two dots
- **Third plinth:** Three dots
- **Fourth plinth:** Four dots

The symbols identify the devices. They do not indicate their current condition.

## INITIAL STATES

The one-dot, two-dot, and three-dot plinths begin active.

Their cylindrical caps are raised.

The four-dot plinth begins inactive.

Its cap is lowered and nearly flush with the surrounding stone.

### ACTIVE PLINTH

- Cap raised
- Identifying symbol visible on top
- Narrow seam illuminated
- Faint sustained vibration

### INACTIVE PLINTH

- Cap lowered
- Symbol recessed
- Seam dark
- No sustained tone

## PLAYER INTERACTION

Activating an inactive plinth raises its cap.

Activating an active plinth lowers it.

Every corner plinth is reversible.

### ACTIVATION CUE

1. The player activates the device.
2. The symbol brightens.
3. The cap rises.
4. A short tone sounds.
5. The corresponding ring on the central plinth fills into a dot.

### DEACTIVATION CUE

1. The symbol dims.
2. The cap lowers.
3. The tone plays in reverse.
4. The corresponding central dot becomes a ring.

## PLAYER EXPERIENCE

The simplest solution is to locate and activate the four-dot plinth.

However, the player may encounter the already active devices first.

They may deactivate one without realizing its importance.

This creates several natural discoveries:

- Interactions can be reversed.
- Apparent progress can be undone.
- The central symbol changes in response to distant events.
- The quadrants correspond to locations.
- All four devices must be active simultaneously.

There is no failure state.

The maze remains solvable regardless of the order in which the devices are found or changed.

---

# SCENE 2F: COMPLETION OF THE FOUR

When the fourth active state is achieved, the player may be anywhere in the maze.

The world responds, but nothing visible emerges from the center.

## DISTANT COMPLETION CUE

- A low tone travels through the ground.
- Dust falls from nearby wall joints.
- A deep mechanical latch sounds somewhere beyond the visible maze.
- The far outer slice shifts by only a fraction of a degree, then settles.
- The sky above that region appears to tremble briefly beneath the blue star.

The hidden obelisks remain dark.

The hidden chamber remains dormant.

No light rises above the maze because the assembly still faces away from the player.

## CENTER PLINTH STATE

All four quadrants now contain solid dots.

```text
●   ●

●   ●
```

The plinth's outer seam begins glowing faintly.

The dots pulse once in clockwise sequence.

Then the entire symbol sinks slightly into the stone.

A cylindrical control rises from the body of the central plinth.

---

# SCENE 2G: THE ROTATION CONTROL

## RAISED CONTROL

The newly raised control is divided by a horizontal seam.

Across its two sections is an incomplete square:

- The lower section shows the bottom portion of a square.
- The upper section shows the top portion, shifted sideways.
- The two portions clearly belong together but remain misaligned.

On the top surface is a new symbol:

> a spiral or swirl

The swirl is the first symbol in the game that communicates motion rather than location or state.

## FIRST CENTRAL ACTIVATION

The first activation of the completed central plinth raises this control.

The mechanism does not yet turn.

This creates a deliberate pause.

The player has satisfied the conditions and uncovered the action they are now permitted to take.

## SECOND DELIBERATE ACTION

The raised control becomes interactive.

Activating it initiates the world twist.

The player therefore performs two separate acts:

1. Establish the correct state across the four corners.
2. Commit that state by turning the central control.

Understanding prepares the world.

Choice moves it.

---

# SCENE 2H: THE HIDDEN FACE TURNS INTO VIEW

## CONTROL ACTIVATION

When the player activates the raised control:

1. The swirl symbol illuminates.
2. The upper half begins rotating.
3. The shifted top of the square moves toward alignment.
4. A deep vibration answers from the far side of the world.
5. The back outer slice begins to move.

The control turns exactly one quarter-turn.

The far slice follows it.

## SCRIPTED WORLD TWIST

The hidden outer slab performs a full 90-degree rotation.

There is no cinematic cut.

The player witnesses the actual world changing configuration.

At first, the moving slice appears to carry only distant maze walls and the outer edge of the world.

As it rotates, another surface comes into view.

Attached to that surface are:

- two obelisks
- the cylindrical portal chamber
- a stone platform
- a corridor formerly facing away from the playable region

The assembly does not rise from inside the world.

It rotates into existence from the player's perspective.

## VISUAL EXPERIENCE

The far maze tilts upward.

Paths become walls.

Walls become ground.

A strip of the world's hidden face rotates across the sky.

The two obelisks first appear sideways along the moving slab. As the quarter-turn continues, they arc upward with their facelets.

The portal chamber moves between them.

At the end of the rotation:

- the hidden face becomes part of the upper playable surface
- the obelisks stand upright relative to their new ground
- the chamber rests between them
- the newly positioned maze corridors connect to the existing route network

The player can now see the destination that had been physically present but spatially unavailable.

## PHYSICAL CUES

- Dust shakes loose from moving walls.
- Loose decorative stones remain attached to their facelets.
- Vegetation turns with the slice.
- Blue-white sunlight shifts sharply across the maze.
- The small dark world remains fixed in the sky, showing that the ground has moved beneath the heavens.
- The camera absorbs a restrained rotational impulse.
- The player remains outside the moving slice.
- The twist concludes with a deep locking sound.

This is not a collapse.

It is a machine executing an ancient, exact motion.

## SYMBOLIC COMPLETION

At the moment the twist finalizes, the two halves of the square on the central control align.

The square becomes complete.

The swirl remains illuminated for several seconds, then fades.

The local symbol and the distant world have performed the same action.

---

## POST-ROTATION SILENCE

For one brief moment after the slice settles, nothing happens.

The newly revealed assembly remains dark.

The player is given time to recognize what has appeared.

Then the obelisks awaken.

## OBELISK ACTIVATION

The effects occur only after the twist has fully finalized.

1. A line of light appears at the base of the left obelisk.
2. It climbs toward the tip.
3. The right obelisk answers in the same manner.
4. Both emit a low synchronized tone.
5. Their inner faces illuminate the chamber between them.
6. A narrow seam becomes visible around the cylinder.

The obelisks act as stabilizers rather than decorative monuments.

Their activation confirms that the rotated slice has locked into the correct orientation.

## CHAMBER ACTIVATION

The cylindrical portal chamber rises slightly from its base.

Its upper and lower sections separate at a horizontal seam.

Light appears between them.

The chamber is already aligned because the world itself has placed it correctly.

The obelisks project narrow fields inward.

A vertical energy veil forms around the cylinder.

The hidden mechanism is no longer merely visible.

It is active.

---

# SCENE 2I: THE NEW PATH

The twist has changed the maze's connectivity.

A route that previously terminated at the far wall now continues onto the rotated slice.

The player must travel through the altered maze to reach the newly revealed portal.

This final walk allows the player to inspect the consequences of the twist:

- former walls now lie beneath their feet
- vegetation grows sideways relative to its previous orientation
- corner structures may be visible on unexpected surfaces
- distant portions of the maze no longer align as they did before
- the central clearing can be seen from a new angle

The portal is therefore both a reward and a destination the player must still approach.

---

# SCENE 2J: THE DOWNWARD PORTAL

## PORTAL ENERGIZATION

Energy flows vertically through the chamber.

It does not rise.

It descends.

Threads of light enter from above, pass through the center, and disappear into the ground.

Particles accelerate as they approach the floor, implying depth rather than impact.

The sound also moves downward:

- high tones begin near the top
- lower tones gather at the base
- the final resonance seems to continue beneath the player's feet

The direction is communicated without text:

> Entering will take the player into the world.

## PORTAL STATE

- Active
- Unsealed
- Downward travel style
- Trigger positioned on the center sub-cell
- No automatic pull
- No return portal visible
- Destination pushed onto the world stack
- Current maze state remains persistent
- Rotated slice remains in its new configuration

The player must step fully into the chamber.

Standing beside it produces no transition.

---

# ENDING IMAGE OF SCENE 2

The player stands on a surface that was invisible when they entered the world.

The two obelisks frame the downward-flowing portal.

Behind them lies the rotated slice, now joined to the maze as walkable ground.

Across the distance, the central clearing and its plinth remain visible.

Above, the blue star remains large and cold. The small dark world is still present in the sky, unexplained.

The journey from the plinth to the portal traces the meaning of the puzzle:

> The player changed four distant states.  
> The world revealed a hidden face.  
> The hidden face contained the way forward.

The scene ends when the player steps into the chamber.

> **BLACKOUT:** None  
> **TRANSITION:** Downward energy streaks envelop the player  
> **TRAVEL MODE:** Nested descent into the same larger world  
> **NEXT WORLD:** Scene 3, an interior world within the ancient maze world  
> **RETURN STATE:** Scene 2 remains twisted and persistent

---

## Builder Presence

No Builder appears in humanoid form.

The world itself acts as an interface.

The four corner plinths ask the player to understand relationships across distance.

The central plinth translates those relationships into a local symbol.

The rotation control turns understanding into physical transformation.

The portal assembly demonstrates that something may be present without being visible, inaccessible without being absent.

The scene may later be understood as a direct expression of Builder perception:

> A thing does not need to be created merely because the observer has not yet seen it.

---

## Design Principles Established

1. **Devices retain and communicate persistent state.**
2. **Interactions may be reversed.**
3. **A local display can represent distant conditions.**
4. **Symbols communicate identity, state, motion, and completion.**
5. **Solving a condition and committing its result are separate actions.**
6. **A world can hide functional structures on inaccessible faces.**
7. **The world itself can rotate as part of a puzzle.**
8. **Props remain attached to their tiles throughout a twist.**
9. **Twisting can reveal new walkable ground and reconnect maze routes.**
10. **Portal machinery activates only after the world reaches the correct orientation.**
11. **The player's understanding does not summon the destination. It makes the destination reachable.**

The first scene ended with the player discovering a doorway.

The second ends with the player discovering that the world had been carrying one all along.
